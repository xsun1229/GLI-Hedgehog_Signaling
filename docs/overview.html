Question

The collaborator studies how GLI-mediated Hedgehog signaling affects neuronal progenitor differentiation. GLI is overexpressed, then perturbed or removed, and gene expression is measured over a time course. Genes that respond were grouped into 10 clusters by their expression pattern (maSigPro).

Hypothesis: genes that respond to GLI perturbation are enriched for genes implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders.

Test

The test is Fisher’s exact test, two-sided, as in the earlier rare-variant analysis. For each cluster and gene set, the 2×2 table compares the cluster with the rest of the background:

in gene set not in gene set
in cluster k a b
rest of background c d

We use two backgrounds, because they answer different questions:

  • All protein-coding genes (19,362 HGNC genes). Is cluster k enriched relative to the genome?
  • All clustered genes (6,433). Is cluster k enriched relative to the other GLI-responsive clusters? This is the stricter test. It partly controls for the fact that genes expressed in neural progenitors are generally enriched for neurodevelopmental genes.

FDR is Benjamini–Hochberg within each background (10 clusters × 7 gene sets). The seven pooled “all clusters vs genome” tests are their own family. A two-sided test flags depletion as well as enrichment, so results always show the direction.

Caveats and next steps

  • Background. The ideal background is every gene measured in the experiment, not every protein-coding gene. If the collaborator can share that list, we will rerun with it.
  • The gene sets overlap. SFARI, TADA and NDD share many genes, so results across gene sets are not independent.
  • Mutation-intolerant genes. Many NDD genes are highly intolerant of damaging mutations. A cluster rich in such genes could look enriched for that reason alone. Next step: a test that accounts for this, using the constraint and haploinsufficiency lists.
  • Interpretation needs the expression pattern of each cluster, e.g. up or down after GLI removal, from the collaborator.
